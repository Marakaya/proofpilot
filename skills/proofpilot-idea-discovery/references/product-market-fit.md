# Product-Market Fit

Load for PMF, customer value, repeat use, monetization, scaling readiness, or an explicit request for Sequoia's approach. Apply only the relevant checks within `discover`, `validate`, `plan`, or `review`; a copy edit does not need a new PMF interview.

This is ProofPilot's adaptation of public methods, not Sequoia's investment score or a program eligibility rule. Methodological sources justify a test design; they do not establish demand for the user's project.

## Choose The Unresolved Question

Sequoia's 2025 framework connects company advantage, customer concern, behavior change, and commercial viability. Reuse the founder-access and problem/commitment evidence already gathered through [discover.md](discover.md) and [validate.md](validate.md). Do not repeat those interviews merely to fill four boxes.

For each decision-relevant claim, connect:

`segment + product + observation period → claim → evidence IDs → contradiction or gap → next discriminating test`

Describe the claim as supported for the next step, contradicted, or unresolved. These are evidence assessments, not additional recommendation tokens. A bounded pilot can support another test without proving durable PMF. Use the shared decision contract for the actual recommendation.

When timing matters, name the observable change that makes the opportunity feasible or attractive now and explain the causal link. A technology becoming fashionable is insufficient. If no enabling change is known, leave timing unresolved; novelty is not mandatory for a useful business.

## Pick A Test For The Customer's Situation

Use Sequoia's 2024 archetypes as provisional hypotheses about a particular segment:

| Customer relationship to the problem | Test priority |
| --- | --- |
| **Hair on Fire:** recognizes urgency and seeks a solution | Test whether the proposed advantage wins against a real alternative and the cost of switching. |
| **Hard Fact:** accepts the problem as normal | Show a credible alternative, then observe whether its value overcomes existing habits and switching effort. |
| **Future Vision:** cannot yet imagine or believe the solution | Demonstrate the uncertain capability and identify a reachable initial use case with a potential buyer. |

Allow mixed and unknown cases. Record the observation behind a classification and what would change it. Revisit it when the segment, product, channel, or customer behavior changes; do not use an archetype to excuse persistently negative evidence. No path automatically establishes or disproves PMF.

## Separate The Signals

| Observation | What it can support | What remains unproven |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Interview, waitlist, expressed interest | Problem or segment hypothesis | Actual use, payment, durable demand |
| Costly commitment: time, data access, pilot, deposit | Willingness to take that specific next step | Successful delivery, repeat behavior, renewals |
| First useful result | Initial value and activation | Return at the next relevant opportunity |
| Repeated useful result | Continuing value in the observed segment | Payment if usage is free |
| First payment | Acceptance of that price and offer | Renewal, regular-price demand after a discount, profitable delivery |
| Renewal or repeat purchase | Continued paid demand over the observed period | Acquisition and delivery economics at larger scale |

These signals may arise in different orders. A deposit is not delivered value; free retention is not paid retention. Before a product exists, use problem, commitment, or feasibility tests instead of requiring unavailable usage history.

## Read Retention Without False Precision

For a live product, define the meaningful value event and its natural recurrence before interpreting retention. Compare cohorts at the same elapsed age; show starting cohort size, returning count, denominator, and observation window. Keep immature cohorts out of comparisons for intervals they have not reached. [Retention](https://articles.sequoiacap.com/retention) motivates the event, frequency, and cohort approach; the following measurement safeguards are ProofPilot's implementation.

Separate users from paying accounts, first payment from renewal, and discounted from regular-price customers. State whether the unit is people, accounts, transactions, or revenue. Identify changes in channel, product, or offer that make cohorts incomparable; inspect a promising segment without silently discarding unsuccessful users. Small counts warrant uncertainty, not confident percentages alone.

An annual workflow cannot demonstrate annual retention in a short pilot. For a genuinely one-off job, use successful delivery, paid conversion, referrals where relevant, and economics; do not manufacture a requirement for daily engagement. A login, notification click, or incentive-driven return is useful only if it represents the promised value.

Before recommending substantial acquisition spending, inspect the available value and retention evidence. Missing history can justify a bounded instrumented test; it does not by itself justify killing the project or scaling it. Choose sample, duration, and pass/fail/inconclusive conditions from the risky assumption, natural frequency, attainable access, and cost of error. Do not import universal interview counts or benchmark percentages.

## Match The Business And Close The Loop

For commercial projects, test who owns the budget, what offer they accept, and whether the price covers delivery; use [ai-product-validation.md](ai-product-validation.md) when AI changes quality or delivery cost. For a local business, use the founder's income and capacity goals. For public goods, distinguish beneficiaries from funders and test sustained use, impact, and a credible funding path. Venture-scale returns are relevant only when that is the objective.

End with the narrowest recommendation the evidence supports, its target, the key uncertainty, and the next test. Preserve positive evidence as well as contradictions; severity of wording is not rigor. After each test, update the claim and relevant segment assumptions. A new segment or business model requires fresh evidence rather than inherited PMF from the old one.
